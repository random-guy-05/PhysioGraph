# Paired ionized calcium and pH after original SpO2 instability

Freeze before new patient calcium counts or values. Scope is the original MIMIC/eICU HF study. All earlier negative results, including the methemoglobin hospital experiment, are known. This is a new biochemical candidate, not a new exposure threshold or a rescue of that experiment.

Hypothesis: early SpO2 instability precedes an ionized-calcium decline accompanied by alkalinization. Blood ionized calcium is physiologically relevant to contraction but does not measure intracellular cardiomyocyte calcium. The [1993 ferret experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8238582/) found a biphasic contractile response to respiratory alkalosis, not a simple progressive loss of contractility. A [1997 ten-volunteer experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9264919/) found no significant arterial ionized-calcium decline during short hyperventilation despite substantial alkalinization. Thus chemistry alone does not establish the proposed patient sequence. Generic calcium prognosis and alkalosis physiology are already known. Positive blood associations would require further evidence before any novel biological or treatment claim.

Use the already validated lowest original stay per person, unchanged SpO2 exposure, pre-ICU window `[max(-1440,hospital_start),0)` and follow-up `(240,min(1680,observed_end)]`. Select the last pre-ICU and first follow-up **ionized-calcium timestamps** before considering values or accompanying pH. Do not replace a selected calcium timestamp after any quality or pH failure.
