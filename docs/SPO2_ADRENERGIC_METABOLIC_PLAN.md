## Biology and scope

In [human epinephrine experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6314140/), beta2 signaling reduced potassium and raised glucose; one protocol produced the metabolic response without tachycardia. A [1991 human infusion study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2065473/) found the same potassium/glucose directions and delayed insulin response. [Experimental hypoxia in healthy men](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15044204/) impaired glucose tolerance for up to 150 minutes, with increased epinephrine. These small experiments do not show that ordinary HF SpO2 fluctuations cause an endogenous catecholamine surge. The proposed joint response is compatible with adrenergic physiology but is not a specific catecholamine measurement. Insulin, dextrose, adrenergic drugs, beta agonists, diuresis, renal replacement, sample hemolysis and other illness processes are competing explanations.

Use only the original MIMIC/eICU HF cohorts and unchanged first-four-hour SpO2 exposure. The original four-hour landmark selection, including early-death/support exclusions, remains; this does not represent all patients present at an episode or an unbiased real-time prognostic risk set. Controls are defined by absence of the original exposure through hour four. No additional patient dataset, mortality endpoint, alternative oxygen definition or newly chosen subgroup is introduced. Both databases and many other project hypotheses have already been explored; this is an explicitly exploratory new candidate, not pristine holdout confirmation or prospective trial registration.

## Qualification and exact joint event

Require all entry times present. At each assay/timestamp use the latest entry; reject conflicting latest native numeric values. Require a single allowed normalized unit tuple among all entries at the timestamp. MIMIC additionally requires all specimen IDs present and one distinct specimen ID per assay/timestamp. Reject result-field inequality prefixes (`<`, `>`, `<=`, `>=`, Unicode variants, 'less than', 'greater than') and any 'hemol' mention in the selected latest result text or comments. This conservative flag can exclude ambiguous/negated comments; absence of such text does not establish freedom from hemolysis, especially in eICU. MIMIC `___` text does not prove that the original assay was uncensored.

Potassium must be finite in [1,10] mEq/L and glucose in [10,1500] mg/dL. Preserve source decimal strings. Require ordinary decimal notation (scientific notation is excluded) and exact representation on a 10^-8 native-unit grid; reject finer nonzero precision rather than making a threshold decision after binary rounding. Compare scaled integers independently in Python and SQL. Clinical interpretation is limited to recorded values.

Define the joint event as **potassium decrease >=0.5 mEq/L AND glucose increase >=20 mg/dL AND glucose follow-up >=1.2 times baseline**. These are investigator-selected substantial-change thresholds, not a validated clinical syndrome or a definition of hypokalemia, and are fixed before values. No threshold sensitivity can rescue failure. Report the eight joint direction/threshold combinations and continuous changes descriptively so that isolated-component changes cannot be mislabeled a coupled event.
